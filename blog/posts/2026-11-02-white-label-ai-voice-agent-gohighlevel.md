A white label ai voice agent gohighlevel agencies can resell is built and run by someone else inside your account, under your brand. On our [white-label voice agent](/white-label-ai-voice-agent/) you pay $400 setup and $250 a month per client, and agencies typically charge $500 to $800 a month.

That leaves $250 to $550 a month per account, before the setup fee. Below: where the margin comes from, where it shrinks, and who should skip it.

## What does a white-label AI voice agent for GoHighLevel cost to resell?

These are the numbers on the [white-label page](/white-label-ai-voice-agent/) and the pricing page.

- **Setup:** $400 per client account, one time.
- **Monthly:** $250 per client account, with 500 call minutes included.
- **Past 500 minutes:** 15 cents a minute.
- **Your price to the client:** your call. Agencies typically land between $500 and $800 a month.

Five accounts at $600 a month bring in $3,000. Your cost is 5 x $250 = $1,250. The margin is $1,750 a month, and that leaves out any markup on the setup fee.

You invoice the client. We do not. We deploy the agent inside your GoHighLevel agency account under your brand, and we never contact your client. The relationship stays with you.

## Where does the margin go wrong?

Those figures are the best case. Three things eat into them.

**Minutes.** 500 minutes is about 250 two-minute calls. For scale, Retell's [published rates](https://www.retellai.com/pricing) for a GPT-4.1 agent add up to $0.13 a minute ($0.055 + $0.015 + $0.045 + $0.015), so 500 raw minutes is about $65 on that platform. Every extra minute costs you 15 cents. If you sold a flat fee with no cap, the overage is yours to absorb. Put a minute allowance in your own contract and pass the overage on.

**Your time.** The agent needs a human who reads the call logs, fields the client's complaints and decides what to change. We fix the technical side. You still own the client. Plan for that time before you price.

**Selling cost.** A $600 line that takes you three sales calls to close is worth less than it looks. Start with clients who already complain about missed calls.

## What does the client get for $500 to $800 a month?

The agent answers in about one second. It asks your client's own qualifying questions, quotes only the prices it was given, books into the GoHighLevel calendar and writes notes to the contact. When a call is missed, the caller gets a text and then an AI callback. Anything urgent, angry or outside the rules goes to a human.

If you want to see how the missed-call flow is built, read [how to build a missed-call AI callback](/blog/retell-gohighlevel-missed-call-ai-callback/).

The client does not need to know what a webhook is (a message one system sends another). They need to see that more calls end in a booking. That is why we measure the baseline first: calls received, calls answered, jobs booked, and the same three numbers 30 days later.

## Why not use GoHighLevel's own voice AI and rebill it?

You can, and for some agencies it is the better choice. It is worth knowing how it works before you decide.

GoHighLevel lets you rebill AI usage to sub-accounts. The [help article](https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/155000003906-ai-employee-access-rebilling-and-reselling) says agencies must be on the $497 a month plan to rebill AI Employee usage. The [pricing page](https://www.gohighlevel.com/pricing) shows the $297 plan rebilling phone and email with no markup, and the $497 plan rebilling with markup.

There are two pricing models.

With markup, the client pays GoHighLevel's cost times a multiplier. With [fixed-rate rebilling](https://help.gohighlevel.com/support/solutions/articles/155000008422-fixed-rate-rebilling-for-ai-products), the client pays a flat rate per minute, and the article's example is $0.10 a minute. Under both, your agency pays GoHighLevel's actual cost.

On a fixed rate you carry the risk: if your rate is below the cost, the article says "You absorb the difference."

The two routes differ like this:

| | Native GoHighLevel voice AI | White-label agent built on Vapi or Retell |
|---|---|---|
| Who builds the agent | You | An engineer |
| Billing | Usage-based, rebilled by you | Flat monthly per account |
| Plan needed | $497 plan to rebill AI usage | Deployed in your agency account |
| Cost you can predict | Varies with usage | $250 plus any overage |

If you have the time to write prompts, test them and watch the logs, native voice AI may be cheaper. If you do not, you are really buying engineering, and that is what the monthly fee covers. Pick based on who will fix the agent at 9pm.

The first build takes five to seven days. After that it becomes a reusable GoHighLevel snapshot (a saved copy of the setup) for the niche, so the next client in the same niche onboards in under an hour. You add their prices, hours and FAQs. The snapshot is where the reselling math improves. The effort sits in the first account. After that, most of the work is entering each client's details.

## Who should not resell this?

An AI phone agent is not the right add-on for every agency.

- **Your clients get few calls.** A business with ten calls a week will not notice the agent. Sell them a text-back or a booking form instead.
- **Your clients are mostly online-only.** If leads come from forms and chat, voice adds little.
- **You have one client.** The effort of learning the stack does not pay back on a single $600 line.
- **Your clients need regulated advice.** Medical, legal and financial answers need strict rules and a human behind the agent. Start with intake and booking only.
- **You are not ready for calls to fail.** An agent will sometimes mishear an address or book a wrong slot. If you cannot take that call from an annoyed owner, wait.

Also check consent rules for AI callbacks with your own lawyer before you switch outbound calls on. Inbound answering is the simpler place to start.

## Checklist before you resell

1. Pick one niche where you already have two or three clients.
2. Pull each client's monthly call count from their phone or GoHighLevel logs.
3. Work out the minutes: calls times average length. Compare it with 500.
4. Set your price, your minute cap and your overage rate in the client contract.
5. Decide who answers the client when the agent gets something wrong.
6. Agree the baseline numbers you will measure for 30 days.
7. Read the [pricing page](/pricing/) so your cost per account is clear.
