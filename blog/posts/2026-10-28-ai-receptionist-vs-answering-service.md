A traditional answering service answers your calls and passes on a message. An AI receptionist can answer the same call and book the job into your calendar before the caller hangs up. For most local service businesses, that difference is the whole decision.

This is not a claim that one is always better. Each fails in a different way, and the right choice depends on what your callers actually want.

## What does an answering service do?

A live answering service is a call centre that answers in your business name. The operator follows a script you supplied, collects the caller's name, number and reason for calling, and sends it to you by text or email.

That is useful. A human picks up, a caller who needs reassurance hears a person, and you find out who called. But the job is not booked. The caller is now waiting for you to call back, and a caller with a leaking pipe does not wait politely. They call the next business on the list.

Some services offer appointment booking or call transfers as an extra. Ask exactly what they can write into your calendar and who is allowed to quote a price. Operators usually serve many businesses at once and know yours only through the script.

## What does an AI receptionist do differently?

An AI phone agent is software that holds the conversation itself. It can check your calendar, offer a real time slot, take the address, write the job into your CRM and text the caller a confirmation, all on the same call.

Two practical differences follow:

- **It answers every call at once.** There is no queue. If three people call during the Friday rush, all three get answered. Our agents answer in about 1 second.
- **It can finish the task, not just record it.** The caller leaves with a booked slot rather than a promise of a callback.

Speed matters here. In our Blue Ridge demo, the missed-call text-back goes out in 8 seconds and the AI callback reaches the customer in 52 seconds. You can [hear a recovery call yourself](https://voicedesk-h31g.onrender.com/agent/recovery).

## Where does an answering service still win?

Be honest about this, because it is where AI agents get oversold.

- **Emotional or sensitive calls.** A person is better for someone who is upset, grieving or frightened. An AI agent should recognise those calls and hand off, not try to comfort.
- **Unusual questions.** A human can improvise on a request nobody planned for. An AI agent follows the script and rules it was given, and the good ones say "let me check and call you back" instead of guessing.
- **Callers who dislike talking to software.** Some customers will hang up the moment they sense a machine. If your customers skew that way, test before committing.
- **Very low call volume.** If you get a handful of calls a week and most are not urgent, neither service may pay for itself. A good voicemail greeting and a prompt callback can be enough.

## Where does the AI receptionist win?

- **Jobs that book on the first call.** Plumbing, HVAC, dental hygiene visits, restaurant tables, viewings. The caller wants a time, and the agent can give one.
- **After-hours and overflow.** Calls arrive when nobody is free to answer, which is exactly when a message-taking service makes the caller wait longest.
- **Consistency.** It asks the same questions every time and writes them in the same format into your CRM.

## What about emergencies?

This is where the two differ most in risk. A human operator can use judgement. An AI agent needs the emergency rules written down and tested. When we ran our test suite against a cheaper model, it missed 5 emergency handoffs in 41 plumbing calls. That is why we test before launch and why we publish [how we test AI phone agents](/blog/how-we-test-ai-phone-agents/). Any provider who cannot tell you how they test emergency handling should not be answering your emergencies.

## Can you use both?

Yes, and for some businesses that is the right answer. Let the AI agent handle bookings, FAQs and routine calls, and route sensitive or complex ones to a human, whether that is your own staff or a live service. The handoff rule is the part to design carefully.

## A checklist for choosing

1. List your last 20 calls. How many wanted a booking, and how many wanted something else?
2. Ask any provider: can it write into my calendar, or only send me a message?
3. Ask what happens when it can't answer. Transfer, callback, or guess?
4. Ask for the real cost per minute and per call past the plan limit, in writing.
5. Listen to it handle a real call, including a rude one.
6. Measure for 30 days: calls answered, jobs booked, jobs lost.
