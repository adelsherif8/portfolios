An AI receptionist can answer a buyer's 9pm call in about a second, ask your qualifying questions, and book a viewing into your calendar. It should not quote a valuation, give advice, or decide who is a serious buyer.

Below: what it asks, why the first call matters in this trade, what it must never say, and when you don't need one.

## What can an AI receptionist do for a real estate agent at 9pm?

It does the part of the call that doesn't need you. The [real estate page](/real-estate/) lists four calls it handles:

- **A sign call or portal enquiry.** It asks about budget, timeline, pre-approval, and whether the buyer has a property to sell. You get the answers on the lead record, not a voicemail and a number.
- **A seller call.** It takes the address, the reason for moving, and the timeline, then books the appraisal.
- **A viewing request.** It books against your calendar, with travel time respected, so it will not put two viewings across town in the same half hour.
- **Everyone else.** Other agents, cold callers and time-wasters are handled and logged. Only real enquiries reach your phone.

The agent asks the questions you wrote. It does not judge the buyer. You read the answers over coffee and decide who gets the first call back.

## Why does the first call matter so much in real estate?

The [National Association of Realtors 2025 profile](https://virginiarealtors.org/2025/12/08/key-takeaways-from-nars-2025-profile-of-home-buyers-and-sellers/) (summarised by Virginia Realtors) reports that 88% of buyers purchased through an agent or broker. 43% used an agent they found through a referral and 18% went back to an agent they had used before.

The [NAR 2026 highlights page](https://www.nar.realtor/research-and-statistics/research-reports/highlights-from-the-profile-of-home-buyers-and-sellers) puts the seller figure at 92% using an agent. Referrals and repeat clients are a large share, so the calls you can least afford to drop are the others: a sign call or portal enquiry from a stranger who has not picked an agent yet.

On speed, the best-known research is old and not specific to property. A 2007 study of more than 15,000 web leads found the odds of reaching a lead within five minutes were about 100 times the odds at 30 minutes ([summary at Callingly](https://callingly.com/blog/state-of-speed-to-lead)). That summary notes it measured reaching a person, not closed sales.

My own demo shows what fast looks like. On the Blue Ridge Plumbing demo, a missed call gets a text in 8 seconds and an AI callback in 52. A portal enquiry has the same clock running while the buyer compares three agents.

Read those as direction, not a forecast for your market.

## What does a 9pm buyer call sound like?

Here is the shape of the call, as a scene and not a client story. The questions are the ones you set, so yours will differ.

A buyer sees your sign at 8:45pm and calls at 9. The agent answers in about a second, says it is an assistant, and asks what they are looking for. A typical script then covers:

1. Which property or area they called about.
2. Budget range.
3. Timeline: this month, this quarter, "just looking".
4. Whether they are pre-approved or paying cash.
5. Whether they need to sell a home first.
6. Which of your open slots suits them for a viewing.

If they ask "what is it worth?" or "will they take less?", the agent says it cannot answer that and offers a call with you. That is a hard rule in the script, because a number quoted on a property nobody has seen can cost you a listing.

Every answer is written to the lead. You wake up to a booked viewing and six facts.

## What should an AI receptionist never say?

Write these rules into the script before launch:

- **No valuations or price advice.** It books the appraisal and takes details.
- **No negotiation.** "Would they take less?" goes to you.
- **No advice on finance, law or schools.** It identifies itself as an assistant, does not advise, and escalates anything it has not been given.
- **No made-up answers.** If the listing detail is not in its knowledge base, it says it does not know and offers a callback. I cover this in [what happens when an AI receptionist doesn't know the answer](/blog/ai-receptionist-doesnt-know-the-answer/).

## When is an AI receptionist not worth it for an agent?

Be honest about these cases:

- **Almost all your business is referrals and repeat clients.** If strangers rarely call, an agent has little to answer. Your own phone is the right tool.
- **You or a team member pick up every call, including evenings.** Then there is nothing to recover.
- **You sell high-end homes where buyers expect you personally.** A first conversation with a machine can feel wrong. Use it for logging and scheduling, or not at all.
- **You get a handful of calls a month.** A text-back and a good voicemail greeting are enough. See [AI receptionist vs answering service](/blog/ai-receptionist-vs-answering-service/) for the cheaper options.
- **You want it to negotiate or advise.** It will not, and should not.
